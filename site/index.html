Why is pH control so hard?

This is an AI project for a Process Dynamics & Controls course: an AI wrote the code, and the student's job is to understand, test and explain every equation and every important line of it. The result is a practical design and simulation tool for an engineer designing or troubleshooting a wastewater pH neutralization system. It shows why pH control is hard — the process gain d(pH)/d(reagent flow) changes by orders of magnitude near the equivalence point — and how engineers deal with it: staged tanks, tank sizing, reagent strength and controller tuning.

What the tool does

  • Computes pH from a charge balance (HCl, optional acetic acid, NaOH reagent; 25 °C, ideal solutions).
  • Plots the titration curve and the process gain versus pH.
  • Simulates 1–3 well-mixed tanks in series, each with a PI loop, valve limits, anti-windup, probe lag and dead time, under step disturbances.
  • Linearizes the model, gives the transfer function, and uses Routh–Hurwitz to show how the stable controller-gain range shrinks near pH 7.
  • Compares 1-, 2- and 3-stage designs by the valve rangeability and resolution each stage needs.
