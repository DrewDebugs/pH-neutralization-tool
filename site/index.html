The problem

Three reasons pH control is hard

1

The cliff

Near equivalence the titration curve is almost vertical. Going from pH 6 to pH 8 takes only 0.072 L/h more caustic: 0.02 % of the 360 L/h caustic flow.

2

A trickle moves it a whole unit

At pH 7 the process gain is about 60,000 pH per m³/h, so roughly 17 mL/h of caustic shifts the steady-state pH by one unit. No valve meters reagent that finely across its whole range.

3

The stable window collapses

Routh–Hurwitz: the largest stable controller gain Kc,u [(m³/h)/pH] falls 5,000× from set point 3 to 7 (log-scale bars). A tuning that is safe at pH 4 is violently unstable at pH 7.
